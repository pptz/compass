# Israeli party compass: research and questionnaire draft

Research cut-off: **29 September 2026**. Version 0.1. This is a reviewable research package, not a deployed product or a fully verified election answer key.

The proposed short form has **10 questions**; the long form uses the same 10 plus another 10. The dataset covers 16 party identities. Evidence is substantially uneven: current detailed plans were accessible for some parties; others yielded historical platforms, brief principles, or only issue-specific statements. Unknown cells remain unknown.

“Similarity to published positions” is a more accurate result label than “compliance with the party program”: some evidence comes from statements rather than a consolidated manifesto. No user answers have been supplied, so this document contains no actual user-to-party percentages.

## Scope and sources

Yashar is interpreted as Eisenkot's ישר! and Beyahad as the Bennett–Lapid ביחד site. Ra'am, Hadash, Ta'al and Balad have separate records. A joint electoral list needs its own agreed answer key; it must not silently inherit every component's positions. Blue and White, United Torah Judaism, Zehut and Reservists are included beyond the user's named examples. This is a party research inventory, not certification of the final ballot or an electoral-viability forecast.

Question order should be mixed across domains. Support and opposition must receive identical visual treatment. Do not show party names beside answers before the user completes the questionnaire. These are policy choices, not moral or personality tests.

The current selection is an editorial draft, not a validated psychometric scale. Questions were chosen for policy significance and breadth, not to reproduce a predetermined party ranking. Some items currently have thin evidence across parties; changing an item requires recoding every party against the exact new wording.

## Party answer key

**P** = party-published program/policy being used as current evidence; **S** = specific attributable statement; **H** = historical document; **R** = secondary characterization; **—** = unknown. An undated live page is not proof of a new 2026 manifesto. P and S denote evidence type, not independent certification of every interpretation.

Values use the same −2 to +2 scale as user answers:
- +2: explicit substantive support.
- −2: explicit substantive opposition.
- +1 / −1: an identifiable narrower or qualified policy.
- 0: an explicitly established middle position; never a placeholder for uncertainty.
- Unknown: no sufficiently specific attributable answer, inaccessible evidence, or unresolved conflict.

Firm support is coded +2; this does not claim to measure a politician's emotional intensity. All values are editorial translations of policies into this questionnaire.

## Scoring specification

### Weights

There are five domains: security/service, democratic institutions, religion/state, economy/welfare, and education/public services. Each has equal total weight before skips. This prevents the five security questions in the long form from outweighing the three religion/state questions.

For question i, set w_i = 1 / n_d, where n_d is the number of questions in its domain in the selected form. Thus each domain has total weight 1 and the whole completed questionnaire has weight 5. In the short form every item has weight 1/2. In the long form the respective domain counts are 5, 4, 3, 4 and 4.

Keep these weights fixed after the form is selected. Skipped answers remove their weight; do not give the remaining questions in that domain extra weight. The score denominator normalizes the remaining total.

### Distance and similarity

For user value u_i and known party value p_i:

    item_similarity = 1 - abs(u_i - p_i) / 4
    party_similarity_percent =
        100 * sum(w_i * item_similarity_i) / sum(w_i)

Both sums include only questions the user answered and for which the selected evidence mode gives the party a known value. If the denominator is zero, the result is unavailable.

Exact agreement gives 100%; one scale step apart 75%; two steps 50%; three steps 25%; opposite endpoints 0%.

For an illustrative four-item equal-weight comparison:
- User: [+2, +1, -2, 0]
- Fictional party: [+2, -1, -2, +2]
- Item similarities: [100%, 50%, 100%, 50%]
- Overall similarity: **75%**.

This fictional example explains arithmetic; it is not a ranking of real parties. Never rescale the best-matching party to 100%, and never force the different parties' percentages to sum to 100.

### Missing evidence and eligibility

Let A be all questions the user actually answered, and K the subset with a usable party answer:

    evidence_coverage_percent = 100 * sum_K(w_i) / sum_A(w_i)

Also display the ordinary count, such as “8 of your 10 answers compared.” This distinguishes weighted coverage from item count.

Recommended overall-result eligibility: the user has answered at least 7 short-form or 12 long-form questions, all five domains are represented in the comparison, there are at least 7 / 12 matched questions, and weighted evidence coverage is at least 70%. These are proposed product thresholds, not a scientific standard.

Below the threshold, display **“Insufficient evidence for an overall percentage”**, with an expandable issue comparison and its coverage. Do not place a 100% match based on one known issue at the top of a ranking.

Even eligible scores can use different subsets. A close difference must not be presented as decisive. For direct party-to-party comparison, offer a recalculation on their common known questions and disclose that subset. The 10- and 20-question scores are different instruments and need not be numerically equal.

Default evidence mode uses P and S. Historical H and secondary R are excluded. An optional research view may show them, prominently labeled; it must not silently promote a historical position to a current one. Do not resolve a missing current position by inserting an old value into a current ranking.

The comparison measures agreement with the selected published policies, not trust, competence, feasibility, coalition behavior, likelihood of implementation, or probability of voting for a party.

### Evidence-completeness bounds (optional)

Missing evidence can also be represented honestly as a mathematical range. For an answered item with an unknown party value:
- greatest possible similarity = 1;
- smallest possible similarity = 1 - max(abs(u_i + 2), abs(u_i - 2)) / 4.

Use those endpoints for missing cells and the known similarity for known cells, then divide the weighted totals by the weight of all answered items. This gives bounds caused by missing answers, not a statistical confidence interval. Do not substitute the midpoint of the range for an evidence-based score.

## Before this becomes a public all-party comparison

The questions and scoring rules are drafted. The remaining substantive task is completing and independently reviewing the answer key, particularly for Likud, Religious Zionism, Otzma, Shas, UTJ and the Arab parties, and confirming which programs belong to the final electoral lists.

Use a second reviewer to check the exact wording and source for each cell; resolve disagreements in a written change log. Preserve document dates and source snapshots when possible. Newer specific official statements can supersede older text, but a general slogan cannot silently supersede a specific policy.

One efficient way to fill gaps is to invite each party to answer the exact 20 statements, with a supporting official reference. That would be a separate, explicitly authorized outreach step; no messages have been sent. A party's failure to respond remains unknown, not a negative score.

Do not infer positions from a coalition partner, a single member's unrelated remarks, demographic stereotypes, or the placement of a party on a left–right scale. Legislative behavior, if added later, should be a separately labeled evidence layer rather than silently described as a manifesto.
