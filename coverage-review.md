## Limits and unresolved questions

- Arabic research was conducted separately for Hadash, Balad, Ta’al and Ra’am. Joint statements are assigned only to their named signatories; coalition membership and voters’ opinions do not establish party policy.
- Balad’s official programme carries a 2017 page date but contains material from different periods. It is kept historical. Continuity on constitutional equality, wealth taxation and collective bargaining is an explicit 60%-weighted inference, not a verified 2026 pledge.
- Undated interviews remain undated. Hadash’s education statement was available through indexed Arabic text; direct retrieval was intermittent. Sector-specific demands establish only a broad spending direction, not a complete nationwide fiscal package.
- Likud’s transport response retains historical exceptions and private operations. The closest existing service value means no general public network; its exceptions are explained in the interface. Yashar’s statehood statement concerns a government it would form, not a permanent final-status framework.
- Religious Zionism’s September 2026 judicial proposals mention an override but do not establish the majority threshold in the retrieved text. Older judicial positions remain historical. Otzma’s official campaign page was reviewed but does not settle our long-term Gaza administration component.
- The same transport survey contains a newer Democrats response that warrants a separate reconciliation with its earlier programme. This targeted pass did not recode that already better-covered party.
- Large gaps remain, especially for Likud, Otzma Yehudit, Ta’al and Religious Zionism. Unknowns still earn zero points and stay in the denominator. Current evidence was not found for every education, labour, climate, marriage or housing component.
